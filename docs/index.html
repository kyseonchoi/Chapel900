The Setup

Built for repeatability.

Stringing Machine
Alpha Pioneer DC Plus Gravity

A manual constant-pull drop-weight platform with fixed clamps and six-point frame support. The emphasis is a controlled, repeatable pull-and-clamp process.

Why it matters
Consistency over speed.

Repeatability comes from controlling the details: mounting, pull sequence, clamp placement, timing, and final inspection. The goal is not the fastest job, but to reproduce the same recipe consistently.

6-point mounting Supports the frame at six contact points during tensioning.
Fixed clamps Each string is clamped independently rather than relying on floating-clamp geometry.
Gravity-release bases Quick clamp-base release for efficient repositioning between pulls.
Linear gripper plus ratchet The string feeds straight into the gripper. As the string elongates, the gripper is held while the bar is re-indexed upward.
Drop-weight constant pull The weight continues responding to string elongation until the bar settles at the target position.
Diamond-dusted clamp teeth Designed for secure grip when clamp pressure is correctly adjusted.
Stringing Standards

A consistent process, string by string.

These are the working standards used at Chapel 900. Manufacturer-specific instructions take priority whenever they are available.

01
One string, one pull Each main and cross is tensioned individually. Double pulling two strings at once is not used.
02
Manufacturer-first patterns Published manufacturer patterns determine start points, skips, tie-offs, and one- or two-piece construction.
03
Starting clamp for clean cross starts A starting clamp is used when appropriate so crosses can be started cleanly without relying unnecessarily on a starting knot.
04
Balanced main progression Main strings are progressed in a balanced sequence to avoid loading one side of the frame excessively.
05
Low-friction cross weaving Crosses are woven at an angle and, when practical, one ahead to reduce friction, heat, and premature notching.
06
Straight before the pull Each string is kept straight while tension is applied, with consistent clamp placement and pull timing.

Tennis and String Tips

A few things worth knowing.

Practical starting points, not absolute rules. Your racquet, string, playing style, and arm should ultimately guide the setup.

⏱

Restring by playing hours

Polyester can lose useful playability before it breaks. A practical starting point is to reassess a full-poly bed at roughly 15 to 20 on-court hours, then adjust based on control, spin, feel, notching, and arm comfort.

Typical schedule Poly check point
2 h × 2 days per week about 4 to 5 weeks
2 h × 3 days per week about 2.5 to 3.5 weeks
2 h × 4 days per week about 2 to 2.5 weeks
🎾

Choose the string for the player

Synthetic gut is a strong default for comfort and value. Polyester makes more sense for players who swing fast enough to benefit from its control and spin behavior. A hybrid can split the difference.

↕

Tension is a recipe, not a measurement

The number on the machine is reference tension. It is not the final tension of every individual string. Start within the racquet manufacturer's recommended range and adjust in small steps from a known baseline.

↔

Synthetic gut can move and may need straightening

Nylon strings often show visible string movement after play. That does not automatically mean the string job is bad. If the strings are still in good condition, occasional realignment by hand is normal.

◎

Gauge affects both durability and feel

Thicker strings generally favor durability, while thinner strings can change feel, responsiveness, and ball bite. However, a thinner string is not automatically softer; the final response also depends on material, construction, and reference tension.

☀

Heat is not your friend

Avoid leaving a strung racquet in a hot car for long periods. Temperature can affect strings and tension, and extreme heat is unnecessary stress for both the stringbed and the frame.

✂

Do not wait for breakage

Loss of control, reduced spin, a dead or harsh response, deep notching, or obvious fraying can all justify restringing before the string actually snaps.

🏆

Before an important match

If you already know a setup you trust, restring early enough to get one or two practice sessions on it. A tournament is usually not the best time to introduce an unfamiliar string or tension.

?

Quick myths

Lower tension means dramatically more power. Usually overstated. Tension can noticeably change feel, launch angle, dwell time, and confidence even when the measured ball-speed difference is fairly modest.
A shaped string automatically creates more spin. Not by shape alone. Ball-to-string friction, string-to-string friction, lateral movement, and snap-back all matter.
If it has not broken, it is still fine. Not necessarily, especially with polyester. Playability can deteriorate well before breakage.
